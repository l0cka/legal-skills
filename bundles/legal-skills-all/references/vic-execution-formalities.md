# Victorian execution formalities reference

Verified against the official publisher, legislation.vic.gov.au, on
20 August 2026. Each entry records the in-force version displayed by the
publisher at verification. This file is a research snapshot: when the
deployment platform allows web access, re-verify every cited provision before
relying on it; when it does not, the responsible solicitor verifies manually
before any document is executed.

The workflow lists these requirements and their statutory basis. It never
determines that a formality has been satisfied — execution, witnessing,
certification and acceptance are acts the solicitor and client perform or
check. Physical and remote execution are alternatives for the solicitor to
select, never defaults chosen by the workflow.

## Will — Wills Act 1997 (Vic)

Official in-force version 036, incorporating amendments as at 26 April 2021
(accessed 20 August 2026).

- **ss 5–6 — minimum age.** A will made by a minor is not valid, subject to
  the marriage-related exceptions in s 6 and court-authorised wills under
  s 20. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 7 — execution.** The will must be in writing and signed by the testator,
  or by another person in the testator's presence and at their direction,
  with testamentary intent. The testator must make or acknowledge the
  signature before at least two witnesses present at the same time, and at
  least two witnesses must attest and sign in the testator's presence.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 8A–8C — remote execution.** Remote execution is permitted only through
  the statutory procedure: all elements occur on the same day and within
  Victoria; one witness acts as the special witness and signs last; the
  transmitted document, identity checks, statements and certifications meet
  s 8A; and the special-witness document is the will under s 8B. Recording
  requires every party's consent. Flag remote execution for the solicitor;
  never conduct or certify it. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 9–11 — dispensing power and witnesses.** Section 9 is a court safety
  net, never a drafting target. A person unable to see and attest the
  testator's signature cannot witness under s 10. Section 11 does not
  disqualify a witness, or that witness's spouse or domestic partner, from a
  benefit, but the relationship remains a solicitor-review flag. Evidence
  state: `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 13–14 — marriage and divorce.** Marriage generally revokes a will,
  subject to s 13 exceptions including contemplation of marriage. Divorce
  revokes specified dispositions, powers and appointments involving the
  former spouse, subject to s 14. Flag intended marriage, marriage, divorce
  and separation; never determine effect. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 39 — survivorship.** Unless a contrary intention appears, a beneficiary
  who dies within 30 days after the testator is treated as predeceasing them.
  Record silence rather than filling the statutory construction rule as an
  instruction. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.

## Enduring power of attorney — Powers of Attorney Act 2014 (Vic)

Official in-force version 007, incorporating amendments as at 26 April 2021;
prescribed forms checked against Powers of Attorney Regulations 2025,
in-force version 001 from 10 August 2025 (accessed 20 August 2026).

- **ss 23 and 28 — principal and attorney eligibility.** The principal must
  be at least 18 and have decision-making capacity for making the power.
  Section 28 sets attorney eligibility, including age, insolvency, relevant
  dishonesty disclosure and service-provider restrictions. Flag facts; the
  solicitor assesses capacity and eligibility. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 30–31 — multiple and alternative attorneys.** Attorneys may act
  jointly, severally, jointly and severally, or by majority; silence defaults
  to joint appointment. Alternatives must be linked to the attorney or
  attorneys they replace. Record instructions or silence, never select or
  import a default into a precedent site. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 32 and Powers of Attorney Regulations 2025 reg 6, sch 1 form 1 —
  prescribed form.** The enduring power and each attorney or alternative
  attorney acceptance must use the prescribed form or be to like effect.
  A firm precedent must be checked against the current form before execution.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 33–36 — execution and witnessing.** The principal, or an eligible
  directed signer, signs before two eligible witnesses. Both witnesses sign
  and date in the principal's and each other's presence and give the required
  certifications. At least one must be authorised to witness affidavits or be
  a medical practitioner; s 35 sets further exclusions. Evidence state:
  `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 5A–5C and 33(2)–(4) — remote witnessing.** Remote witnessing is
  permitted only through the statutory procedure, including same-day and
  within-Victoria requirements, a special witness who signs last, document
  transmission and certifications, and consent to any recording. Flag for
  solicitor handling; never conduct or certify it. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 37–38 — acceptance.** Each attorney and alternative attorney must sign
  the prescribed acceptance in the instrument. Unless the appointee is a
  trustee company, an adult witness must witness and sign the acceptance.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.

## Appointment of medical treatment decision maker — Medical Treatment Planning and Decisions Act 2016 (Vic)

Official in-force version 012, incorporating amendments as at 1 July 2024;
prescribed details checked against Medical Treatment Planning and Decisions
Regulations 2018, in-force version 001 from 12 March 2018 (accessed
20 August 2026).

- **ss 26–28 and regulation 6 — appointment.** An adult with decision-making
  capacity may appoint another adult. The appointment must be written in
  English, state the appointor's full name, date of birth and address, state
  each appointee's name, date of birth and address, be signed, witnessed and
  certified, and be accepted before taking effect. If more than one appointee
  is listed, the first reasonably available, willing and able person acts.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 29 — acceptance.** Each appointee signs an acceptance on the same
  document containing the statutory statements, including having read and
  understood any advance care directive already given or given at the same
  time. An adult witnesses and certifies the acceptance signature. Evidence
  state: `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 3, 36–37 — witnessing and directed signing.** Two adult witnesses sign
  and date in the presence of the appointor and each other; at least one is an
  authorised witness and records the qualification. Each witness gives the
  certifications in s 36 and cannot be an appointee. An eligible adult who is
  neither witness nor appointee may sign at the appointor's direction.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.

## Out of scope, always flagged

Advance care directives, supportive attorney appointments, superannuation
death benefit nominations, jointly held property passing by survivorship, and
interstate recognition questions are identified for the solicitor and never
drafted or resolved.
