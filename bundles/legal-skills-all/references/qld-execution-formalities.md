# Queensland execution formalities reference

Verified against the official publisher, legislation.qld.gov.au, on
26 August 2026. Each entry records the version line displayed by the
publisher at verification (the "Current as at" line on the authorised
reprint). This file is a research snapshot: when the deployment platform
allows web access, re-verify each provision against legislation.qld.gov.au
before relying on it; when it does not, the responsible solicitor verifies
manually before any document is executed.

The workflows list these requirements and identify their statutory basis.
They never determine that a formality has been satisfied — witnessing,
execution and certification are physical-world acts the solicitor and
client perform and the solicitor checks.

## Will — Succession Act 1981 (Qld)

Version line at verification: "Current as at 28 April 2026" (accessed
26 August 2026).

- **s 9 — minimum age.** A will made by a minor is not valid, subject to
  the exceptions in s 9(2) (contemplation of marriage; married or
  formerly married minors) and court-authorised minors' wills under s 19.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 10 — execution.** A will must be in writing and signed by the
  testator, or by someone else in the presence of and at the direction of
  the testator; the signature must be made or acknowledged by the testator
  in the presence of 2 or more witnesses present at the same time; and at
  least 2 of those witnesses must attest and sign in the presence of the
  testator, but not necessarily in the presence of each other. Witnesses
  need not know the document is a will; signatures need not be at the
  foot; the testator's signature must be made with the intention of
  executing the will; an attestation clause is not required. A person
  who cannot see and attest that the testator has signed may not act as a
  witness (s 10(10)). Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 11 — interested witnesses.** A disposition to an attesting witness
  (or a person claiming under them) is void unless at least 2 attesting
  witnesses are not interested, all persons who would benefit from the
  avoidance consent in writing, or the court is satisfied under
  s 11(3)(c). Flag any witness named anywhere in the instruction record.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 13 — revocation.** A will may be revoked only under ss 14–15B, by a
  court-authorised instrument, by a later will, by a document declaring
  an intention to revoke that is executed as a will, or by destruction or
  dealing with the will with intent to revoke. Evidence state:
  `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 14–15B — marriage, civil partnership, divorce and de facto
  relationships.** Marriage (s 14) and entry into a civil partnership
  (s 14A) revoke a will, subject to dispositions to and appointments of
  the spouse or partner and to contemplation exceptions. Divorce or
  annulment (s 15), termination of a civil partnership (s 15A) and the
  ending of a de facto relationship (s 15B) revoke dispositions to,
  appointments of and powers in favour of the former partner unless a
  contrary intention appears. Flag any instruction mentioning an intended
  marriage or partnership, divorce or separation; never determine effect.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 18 — dispensing power.** The court may treat a document that has not
  been executed under Part 2 as a will, alteration or revocation if
  satisfied the deceased intended it to operate as such — a safety net for
  the court, never a drafting target. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 33B — survivorship.** Unless a contrary intention appears, a
  beneficiary who dies within 30 days after the testator is treated as
  having died immediately before the testator. Where instructions are
  silent, record the silence — the statutory default is a construction
  rule, not a value to fill. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **Remote witnessing — none in force.** The Succession Act 1981 contains
  no audio-visual or remote witnessing provision. The temporary scheme in
  the Justice Legislation (COVID-19 Emergency Response—Documents and
  Oaths) Regulation 2020 (SL 2020 No 72, as amended by SL 2020 No 78) was
  repealed on 30 April 2022 by 2021 Act No 23 s 57 (publisher reprint
  note: "This is the last reprint before repeal. Repealed on 30 April
  2022"), and the enabling COVID-19 Emergency Response Act 2020 expired
  on 31 October 2024 (s 24AA(2)). Nothing permanent replaced remote
  witnessing for wills; a Queensland will is executed in physical
  presence under s 10. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.

## Enduring power of attorney — Powers of Attorney Act 1998 (Qld)

Version line at verification: "Current as at 28 April 2026" (accessed
26 August 2026). Approved forms checked against the Queensland Government
publications portal dataset "Power of attorney and advance health
directive forms" (dataset last modified 13 July 2026; accessed 26 August
2026).

- **s 32 — creation.** An adult principal may, by enduring power of
  attorney, authorise one or more eligible attorneys for financial or
  personal (including health) matters; the power is not revoked by the
  principal's later impaired capacity. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 41 — capacity.** The principal has capacity only if capable of
  making the power freely and voluntarily and understanding its nature
  and effect, including the s 41(2) matters (scope and limits, when the
  power begins, the attorney's control, revocability, continuation after
  impaired capacity). Flag facts; the solicitor assesses capacity.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 29 — eligible attorney.** An individual with capacity who is at
  least 18, is not and has not been within the previous 3 years a paid
  carer for the principal, is not a health provider for the principal,
  is not a service provider for a residential service where the principal
  resides, and, for a financial matter, is not bankrupt or taking
  advantage of bankruptcy laws; or the public trustee or a trustee company
  (financial matters only); or the public guardian (personal matters
  only). Flag, never assess. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 43 — multiple, alternative and successive attorneys.** Attorneys may
  be appointed for all or particular matters, jointly, severally, jointly
  and severally, by a stated number, or as alternative or successive
  attorneys; no more than 4 joint attorneys for a matter. Record
  instructions or silence, never select a default. Evidence state:
  `VERIFIED`.
- **s 44(1) and approved forms.** An enduring power of attorney must be
  in an approved form (chief executive approval under s 161; substantial
  compliance suffices). Current approved forms: Form 2 (short form)
  Version 4 and Form 3 (long form) Version 4, each "approved for use from
  30 November 2020"; revocation is Form 6 Version 1. A firm precedent
  must be checked against the current published form before execution.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 44(3)–(5) — execution and witness certificate.** The principal, or
  an eligible signer (s 30: at least 18, not the witness, not an attorney)
  at the principal's instruction and in the principal's presence, signs;
  an eligible witness signs and dates. The witness certifies that the
  principal signed in the witness's presence (or instructed the signer,
  who signed in the presence of both) and appeared to have the capacity
  necessary to make the document. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 31 — eligible witness.** A justice of the peace, commissioner for
  declarations, notary public or lawyer who is not the person signing
  for the principal, not an attorney of the principal, not a relation of
  the principal or of an attorney, and, where the document gives power
  for a personal matter, not a paid carer or health provider of the
  principal. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 44(8) — acceptance.** The document is effective in relation to an
  attorney only if the attorney has accepted the appointment by signing
  it. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 50, 52–53A — revocation.** A later enduring document revokes an
  earlier one to the extent of inconsistency (s 50). Marriage (s 52) or a
  civil partnership (s 52A) revokes the document to the extent it gives
  power to someone other than the new spouse or partner, absent contrary
  intention; divorce (s 53) or termination of a civil partnership (s 53A)
  revokes it to the extent it gives power to the former spouse or partner.
  A written revocation must be in the approved form and witnessed under
  s 49. Flag; never determine effect. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **Remote witnessing — none in force.** The only audio-visual link
  provision in the Act concerns corporate sealing (s 24H). The COVID-era
  remote witnessing scheme for enduring documents was repealed on
  30 April 2022 (see the will entry above). Execution is in physical
  presence. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 60 and Land Title Act 1994 ss 132–134 — registration.** An enduring
  power of attorney may be registered with the Titles Registry; an
  instrument executed under a power of attorney may be registered only if
  the power is registered (Land Title Act 1994 s 132, "Current as at
  1 August 2025"). Once registered, the power continues to authorise land
  dealings until a revocation is registered (s 60(3)). Flag where
  instructions mention real property; registration is a solicitor action.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **Oversight.** The Supreme Court (ch 6) and, concurrently, the
  Queensland Civil and Administrative Tribunal (Guardianship and
  Administration Act 2000 s 82(2), "Current as at 28 April 2026") may
  declare capacity (s 111), declare validity (s 113), and remove or
  replace an attorney (s 116). The Public Guardian may be appointed
  attorney for personal matters (s 29(1)(c)). Identify only; never advise
  on applications. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.

## Advance health directive — Powers of Attorney Act 1998 (Qld), Form 4

Version line at verification: "Current as at 28 April 2026" (accessed
26 August 2026). Approved form: Form 4 Version 5, "approved for use from
30 November 2020"; explanatory guide Form 10 Version 1.

- **ss 35, 42 and 44(2), (6)–(7) — instrument and doctor certificate.** An
  adult with capacity under s 42 may give directions about future health
  matters and appoint an attorney for health matters. The directive must
  be written, may be in the approved form, is signed and witnessed as for
  an enduring power of attorney (with the added s 31(1)(f) exclusion of a
  beneficiary under the principal's will as witness), and must also
  include a certificate signed and dated by a doctor or nurse practitioner
  who is not the witness, signer, an attorney, a relation of either, or a
  beneficiary under the will, stating the principal appeared to have the
  necessary capacity. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **Scope.** Advance health directives are identified and flagged for the
  solicitor. They are not drafted by the workflow: the instrument records
  clinical directions that require medical consultation and a medical
  certificate, and a later directive prevails over an inconsistent
  enduring power of attorney for health matters (s 50 note). Evidence
  state: `VERIFIED`.

## Out of scope, always flagged

Advance health directives (Form 4) are named above but never drafted.
Superannuation death benefit nominations (Commonwealth law), jointly held
property passing by survivorship, statutory health attorney arrangements,
QCAT guardianship and administration applications, and interstate
recognition questions (s 34) are identified for the solicitor and never
drafted or resolved.
