# Legal professional privilege carve-outs

Statutory protections for privileged information under the *Anti-Money
Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006* (Cth), as amended. This
reference maps the mechanics; it never supports a conclusion that privilege
applies or is lost in a given case. Every section number is a routing lead —
verify through `$check-commonwealth-legislation` at use time. AUSTRAC's LPP
guidance page (last updated 1 July 2026 at the snapshot date) is the
regulator's interpretation:
<https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/your-amlctf-program/reporting-us/legal-professional-privilege>.

## The protection

- Section 242(1): the Act does not affect a person's right to refuse to give
  information, or produce a document, that would be privileged from being
  given or produced on the grounds of legal professional privilege.
- Section 5 defines LPP to include, but not be limited to, privilege under
  division 1 of part 3.10 of the *Evidence Act 1995* (Cth). The Act does not
  create a new category of privilege and does not extend privilege to
  communications made for an illegal or improper purpose.
- Privilege is narrower than a practitioner's general duty of
  confidentiality. A confidentiality duty alone does not excuse a reporting
  obligation.

## The LPP form mechanism

Where information a reporting entity must give AUSTRAC is subject to LPP and
is withheld, an LPP form — a written notice in the form approved by the
AUSTRAC CEO stating the basis of the claim — must be submitted in place of
the withheld information. AUSTRAC states that describing withheld
information in an LPP form does not of itself waive privilege, and that
failure to submit a required LPP form may attract civil penalties. Two forms
exist: one for reporting obligations (lodged with the SMR or TTR through
AUSTRAC Online) and one for notices and requests for information (lodged by
email).

Obligations AUSTRAC identifies as carrying an LPP-form requirement include
sections 26Q(3) (program documentation requests), 41(4) (SMRs), 43(4)
(TTRs), 49(3) (further-information notices), 49B(7) (CEO information
notices), 50(7), 75N(4) and 76Q(4) (register decisions), 167(6) and 202(6)
(compliance and designated-service notices).

## Suspicious matter reports and privilege

- Section 41(2A): the SMR obligation does not require giving privileged
  information.
- If **all** of the information grounding the suspicion is privileged, no
  SMR and no LPP form is required.
- If **part** of the information is privileged, the entity must lodge an SMR
  containing the non-privileged information and an LPP form for the
  withheld part.
- Timing per AUSTRAC guidance: terrorism-financing suspicions keep the
  24-hour timeframe; for other suspicions the SMR-with-LPP-form timeframe is
  5 business days from forming the suspicion, extended from the ordinary 3
  business days.
- A practitioner claiming LPP in an SMR context can only do so where the
  privilege belongs to another person, typically the client.

## Threshold transaction reports and privilege

If information ordinarily included in a TTR is privileged, the LPP form is
lodged with the TTR within the 10-business-day TTR timeframe.

## Pending layer

Section 242A provides for ministerial guidelines on the handling of LPP
claims — review and challenge processes, further-information requests,
search handling and dispute resolution. At the snapshot date the guidelines
were announced but not released: a watch item in the
[watch register](../skills/track-aml-ctf-developments/references/watch-register.json),
never a current obligation.

## Human control

Whether a specific communication is privileged, whether privilege is waived
and whether to withhold information are legal judgments for the responsible
lawyer, made if in doubt with independent legal advice. The workflows may
map which obligations carry an LPP-form requirement and flag privilege
questions; they never answer them.
