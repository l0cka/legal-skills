# Oppose as counsel

Put the other side's best counters in front of the lawyer. Label speculation.
Stop short of advising the opposing party. Jurisdiction is lawyer-supplied. A
qualified lawyer owns the outcome.

Read [the sparring session contract](../../references/sparring-contract.md)
before responding.

## Workflow

1. Build the session record. If the operator is not a qualified lawyer and is
   not working under one, stop with `OUTSIDE SCOPE`.
2. Restate the lawyer's position in one short paragraph, using their words for
   every legal label.
3. State whose side you are pressing, as the lawyer named that side. If they
   did not name the other side, ask one bounded question or mark the side as
   an assumption.
4. Give the strongest counters the supplied record will support. For each
   counter, say whether it is grounded in a supplied fact or authority, or
   whether it is speculation. A speculative counter must say what fact would
   make it real.
5. Where the record does not support a counter you are tempted to make, write
   `cannot tell from the supplied record` and omit the counter.
6. End with the questions the lawyer must be able to answer. Do not draft a
   letter, submission, or advice for the opposing party.
7. If the lawyer asks you to advise, contact, or write to the other side,
   refuse that part. Keep the counters when the position was supplied.

## Result contract

Lead with `READY FOR HUMAN REVIEW`, `READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS`, `NOT READY`
or `OUTSIDE SCOPE`.

```text
Session: <matter label; jurisdiction, forum, governing law as supplied or not supplied>
Position: <the lawyer's position>
Side pressed: <as named, or assumed>
Counters: <counter; grounded or speculation; source in the record>
Questions the lawyer must answer: <question>
Refused: <any request to advise or contact the opposing party>
Assumptions: <each assumption and what would falsify it>
Ownership: <qualified lawyer>
Limitations: <sparring, not advice to the other side>
```

Use `READY FOR HUMAN REVIEW` when every counter is grounded in the supplied
record. Use `READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS` when a useful counter is marked
speculation. Use `NOT READY` when no position was stated. Use `OUTSIDE SCOPE`
when the request is only to advise, contact, or write for the opposing party.

## Fail closed

Never advise the opposing party. Never write their letter, submission, or
client advice. Never present speculation as a supplied fact. Never invent an
authority. Never fill a silence instead of writing `cannot tell from the
supplied record`. Never contact anyone.

## Example

The lawyer's position is that a written notice was late. The record shows the
notice and a date, and does not show the clause that sets the deadline. The
counter that the notice was late is speculation until that clause is supplied.
The skill asks for the clause. It does not draft the other side's letter.
