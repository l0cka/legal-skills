# NSW execution formalities reference

Verified against the official publisher, legislation.nsw.gov.au, on
20 August 2026. Each entry records the version line displayed by the
publisher at verification. This file is a research snapshot: when the
deployment platform allows web access, re-verify each provision against
legislation.nsw.gov.au before relying on it; when it does not, the
responsible solicitor verifies manually before any document is executed.

The workflows list these requirements and identify their statutory basis.
They never determine that a formality has been satisfied — witnessing,
execution and certification are physical-world acts the solicitor and
client perform and the solicitor checks.

## Will — Succession Act 2006 No 80 (NSW)

Version line at verification: "Current version for 14 July 2023 to date
(accessed 20 August 2026)".

- **s 5 — minimum age.** A will made by a minor is not valid, subject to
  the exceptions in s 5(2) (contemplation of marriage; married or
  previously married minors) and court-authorised minors' wills under
  s 16. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 6 — execution.** A will is not valid unless (a) it is in writing and
  signed by the testator, or by some other person in the presence of and
  at the direction of the testator; (b) the signature is made or
  acknowledged by the testator in the presence of 2 or more witnesses
  present at the same time; and (c) at least 2 of those witnesses attest
  and sign the will in the presence of the testator, but not necessarily
  in the presence of each other. The signature must be made with the
  intention of executing the will; it need not be at the foot; an
  attestation clause is not essential. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 7 — witness knowledge.** A witness need not know the document is a
  will. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 8 — dispensing power.** The Court may treat an informally executed
  document as a will — a safety net for the Court, never a drafting
  target. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 9 — who cannot witness.** A person unable to see and attest that the
  testator has signed cannot act as a witness. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 10 — interested witnesses.** A beneficial disposition to an
  attesting witness is void unless at least 2 attesting witnesses are not
  interested, all affected beneficiaries consent in writing, or the Court
  is satisfied under s 10(3)(c). Flag any witness named anywhere in the
  instruction record. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 11–13 — revocation.** A will is revoked by, among other things,
  marriage (s 12, with contemplation-of-marriage exceptions) and altered
  in effect by divorce or annulment (s 13). Flag any instruction
  mentioning an intended marriage, divorce or separation. Evidence
  state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 35 — survivorship.** Unless a contrary intention appears, a
  beneficiary who dies within 30 days after the testator is treated as
  having predeceased. Where instructions are silent, record the silence —
  the statutory default is a construction rule, not a value to fill.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.

## Enduring power of attorney — Powers of Attorney Act 2003 No 53 (NSW)

Version line at verification: "Current version for 2 March 2025 to date
(accessed 20 August 2026)".

- **s 19(1) — creation.** An instrument creates an enduring power of
  attorney only if (a) it is expressed to be given with the intention that
  it will continue to be effective even if the principal lacks capacity
  through loss of mental capacity after execution; (b) the principal's
  execution is witnessed by a prescribed witness who is not an attorney
  under the power; and (c) a certificate by that witness is endorsed on or
  annexed to the instrument stating the five matters in s 19(1)(c)
  (explanation given, apparent understanding, prescribed-witness status,
  not an attorney, witnessed the signing). Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 19(2) — prescribed witness.** Includes a registrar of the Local
  Court, an Australian legal practitioner, qualifying licensed conveyancers
  and NSW Trustee and Guardian or trustee-company employees who have
  completed an approved course, certain foreign-qualified practitioners
  instructed independently, and persons prescribed by the regulations.
  Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 20 — acceptance.** The power confers no authority on an attorney
  until the attorney accepts the appointment by signing the instrument;
  with multiple attorneys, authority is conferred only in relation to
  those who accept; this extends to substitute attorneys. Evidence
  state: `VERIFIED`.
- **Prescribed form.** The form of a prescribed power of attorney is set
  by the regulations, which were not verified for this snapshot. Evidence
  state: `VERIFIED WITH QUALIFICATIONS` (form-prescription) — check the
  current Powers of Attorney Regulation before execution.

## Appointment of enduring guardian — Guardianship Act 1987 No 257 (NSW)

Version line at verification: "Current version for 27 March 2021 to date
(accessed 20 August 2026)".

- **s 6 — who may appoint.** A person of or above 18 years may, by
  instrument in writing, appoint a guardian. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 6B — appointee eligibility.** An appointee must be 18 or over and
  must not relevantly provide paid medical, accommodation or daily-living
  support services to the appointor (nor be close family of such a
  provider). Flag, never assess. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 6C — method of appointment.** The instrument must be in or to the
  effect of the form prescribed by the regulations; signed by the
  appointor (or an eligible signer at the appointor's instruction, in
  their presence); endorsed with the appointee's acceptance; witnessed by
  one or more eligible witnesses; and each witness must certify voluntary
  execution and apparent understanding (plus the eligible-signer
  certification where applicable). Appointor and appointee execution may
  be separately witnessed at different times. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`,
  with the prescribed form itself `VERIFIED WITH QUALIFICATIONS`
  (form-prescription) — check the current Guardianship Regulation before
  execution.
- **s 5 — eligible witness.** An Australian legal practitioner, a
  registrar of the Local Court, or a person prescribed by the regulations
  — and, for these instruments, not an appointee or substitute enduring
  guardian. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **ss 6D–6DA — multiple and substitute guardians.** Two or more enduring
  guardians may be appointed jointly, severally, or jointly and severally,
  with different functions each; a substitute requires their own endorsed
  acceptance. Evidence state: `VERIFIED`.
- **s 6E — functions.** The statutory functions (place of residence,
  health care, personal services, Part 5 medical and dental consent, other
  specified functions) may be limited or excluded by the instrument — use
  as the checklist for `guardianship_functions`. Evidence state:
  `VERIFIED`.

## Out of scope, always flagged

Advance care directives (no NSW statutory instrument verified here),
superannuation death benefit nominations (Commonwealth law), jointly held
property passing by survivorship, and interstate recognition questions are
identified for the solicitor and never drafted or resolved.
