# Original commercial clause bank

## Use and provenance

All wording below was authored for this plugin. No licensed precedent text has
been copied. These are optional first-draft examples, not approved firm positions,
legal authority or complete documents. Adapt only to evidenced instructions;
show alternatives and unresolved choices to the qualified lawyer. Apply the
[source and control method](drafting-source-and-control-method.md) and
[UCT checklist](unfair-contract-terms-checklist.md) before use. Do not infer
legal effectiveness from inclusion in this bank. No example sets a default cap,
term, notice period or governing law. Every `<value>` must be supported or become
a visible review marker with an issues-register entry.

## NDA: purpose, exclusions and permitted disclosure

> The recipient may use information disclosed by the discloser in connection
> with <defined transaction> only to <specific evaluation purpose>. Confidential
> Information includes non-public information in any form disclosed for that
> purpose and material derived from it that reveals it. It excludes information
> the recipient can demonstrate was already lawfully known without a duty of
> confidence, becomes public without a breach, is obtained lawfully from an
> independent source without a duty of confidence, or is independently developed
> without using the disclosed information. The recipient must protect it with
> reasonable care and limit access to representatives who need it for the purpose
> and are bound by confidentiality duties. The recipient is responsible for those
> representatives' compliance with these obligations.

For mutual NDAs, each party is a discloser or recipient according to the actual
flow; one-way NDAs must identify the single discloser. Confirm affiliate coverage
and retrospective disclosures separately; never backdate the agreement.

> If disclosure is required by law or a binding regulatory or court requirement,
> the recipient may disclose only what is required. To the extent lawful, it must
> first notify the discloser and reasonably assist efforts to protect the material.
> Nothing in this agreement restricts a protected disclosure or lawful report to
> a regulator.

Verify the Corporations Act 2001 Part 9.4AAA on legislation.gov.au where relevant
to protected disclosures. Check eligibility, recipients and confidentiality
protections before adding notice or consent requirements.

## NDA: duration, return, no licence and relief

> Disclosures are covered during <disclosure period>. The recipient's use and
> confidentiality obligations continue for <agreed survival period or defined
> trade-secret alternative>. On <return trigger>, the recipient must return or
> securely destroy the information and confirm completion on request. Copies
> required by law and inaccessible routine backups may be retained only for those
> purposes, remain protected, and must not be used for a new purpose. No ownership
> or licence is granted except the limited permission to use the information for
> the stated purpose. Either party may seek urgent injunctive or other relief
> from a competent court; granting relief remains for the court to decide.

Choose an evidenced duration and backup/retention process; no promise of guaranteed
injunctions, absolute deletion or unlimited secrecy for all information.

## Consumer rights and subscription mechanics

> Your rights under laws that cannot lawfully be excluded, including applicable
> Australian Consumer Law guarantees and remedies, are preserved. No cap,
> exclusion, cancellation rule or refund restriction in these terms limits
> those rights. Contact <support channel> to report a service problem or request
> a remedy; this process does not restrict your legal remedies.

Verify ACL Schedule 2 ss 60–64A and 267–269 on legislation.gov.au before applying
this wording. Verify ss 18 and 29(1)(m) for misleading statements about the
existence, exclusion or effect of guarantee rights and remedies. Do not use
a savings clause to excuse contradictory exclusions or refund/support messages.

> Before you subscribe we will show the price, billing interval, initial term,
> trial conversion terms and whether renewal is automatic. If you choose
> <automatic renewal arrangement>, we will notify you through <channel> at least
> <notice period> before renewal, including the next price and how to cancel.
> You may cancel through <accessible process> before <cut-off>. Cancellation
> takes effect on <effective date>; access and any refund for unused prepaid
> service are handled under <agreed rule>, subject to your preserved legal rights.

> We may change these terms only for <defined reasons>. We will give you at
> least <notice period> notice through <channel> before a change takes effect.
> If a change materially disadvantages you, you may cancel before it takes
> effect without an exit penalty and receive a refund for unused prepaid service
> after cancellation. Changes do not alter accrued rights or charges retrospectively.

The notice periods and cancellation/refund choices require instructions and UCT
review. Confirm the business can implement the offered process and keep records
of pre-contract disclosure, acceptance and notices. The example is an instructed
commercial option, not proof of statutory compliance.

For intended use from 1 July 2027, verify the [2026 unfair trading Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00064/asmade/text):
enacted, commences 1 July 2027. Adapt the offer flow to future s 48D disclosure,
ongoing information to s 48E and applicable regulations, and cancellation to
s 48F, including its online-exit trigger. Check subscription coverage/exclusions
and cohort tests in ss 48B–48C and 48G–48H. Under s 312, ss 48B, 48E and 48F
also reach older contracts renewed, extended, otherwise continued or varied on
or after commencement, from the effective change date. Assess s 48D separately.
Review pricing displays against future s 48A and conduct against s 28B and
s 311. Until commencement, label this as preparation and apply current law.
Keep statutory requirements, instructed extra protections and unresolved
implementation choices separate; do not invent prescribed reminder intervals.

## Services: SOW, GST and IP alternatives

> Services, deliverables, acceptance criteria, dependencies and milestones are
> set out in each agreed statement of work. A change requires both parties'
> written agreement identifying its effect on scope, fees and timing. Conflicting
> documents are resolved in the order <agreed precedence>.

> Fees are <amount and basis>. Subject to applicable law, GST properly payable
> on a taxable supply is added where the agreed price is GST-exclusive and the
> supplier provides a valid tax invoice. The parties must account for applicable
> adjustments. Invoice timing, payment dates and disputed amounts follow <agreed
> procedure>.

Check the GST Act on legislation.gov.au where relying on tax requirements;
an ABN does not establish GST registration or justify charging GST. Verify ACL
s 48 single-price requirements and exceptions for customer-facing price displays,
including quantifiable taxes/charges; GST-exclusive B2B wording is not a
universal permission to display a price without GST.

Choose one deliverable approach; do not silently combine them:

> **Ownership option:** Subject to <agreed transfer trigger>, the supplier assigns
> to the customer the intellectual property rights it owns in the defined
> deliverables. Background material remains with its owner. The supplier grants
> <specified licence> to background material incorporated into deliverables to
> allow <specified customer uses>. Third-party components and their licence
> conditions must be identified in the SOW before inclusion.

> **Licence option:** Deliverable ownership remains with <owner>. That owner
> grants the customer a <scope, territory, duration, exclusivity, transfer and
> sublicensing rights> licence for <specified uses>. Background and third-party
> materials are identified separately with the rights needed for those uses.

Verify the Copyright Act ss 196–197 on legislation.gov.au for assignment mechanics
and future rights. Confirm employee/subcontractor chain of title; a supplier can
transfer only rights it holds or obtains.

> The supplier must obtain written consents from the relevant individual creators
> for <specified acts or omissions, works and beneficiaries>, to the extent
> permitted by law, and provide evidence of them on request. Attribution and
> editing requirements are <agreed requirements>.

Verify Copyright Act Part IX, including s 195AW (films/works in films),
s 195AWA (other works) and s 195AWB (invalidated consents), on legislation.gov.au.
Moral rights are not assigned with copyright; a company cannot supply an
individual's consent merely by promising that all rights are waived.

## Liability, indemnity, suspension and exit

> Subject to the following carve-outs, each party's aggregate liability for
> <defined claims and period> is limited to <agreed amount or formula>. The cap
> does not apply to liability that cannot lawfully be limited or excluded, or
> to <specifically agreed carve-outs>. The cap's application to indemnities,
> confidentiality, privacy, IP and payment obligations is <express allocation>.

Consider fraud, wilful misconduct and personal injury, plus appropriate IP,
confidentiality and privacy treatment. State whether they are uncapped, separately
capped or within the general cap; preserve non-excludable rights in every case.
Never choose a cap automatically from risk appetite or insurance limits.

> <Indemnifying party> indemnifies <protected party> for <defined losses and
> third-party claims> caused by <specific trigger>, reduced to the extent caused
> by the protected party's own acts or omissions. The protected party must give
> prompt notice, reasonably mitigate loss and permit <agreed defence control>,
> with no settlement imposing obligations on it without consent. Cap treatment
> is <express allocation>.

> Suspension is limited to <objective grounds> and the affected services. Except
> where immediate action is reasonably required for <defined urgent risk>, the
> supplier must give notice and <cure opportunity>. It must restore service when
> the ground is resolved. Termination rights, notice and cure periods are
> <agreed triggers>. On exit the parties settle accrued amounts, refund <agreed
> unused prepaid sums>, provide <transition/data export assistance>, and retain
> only obligations expressly identified to survive.

Inspect proportionality, legitimate interests and effective customer remedies;
these examples do not establish fairness.

## Disputes, law and document mechanics

> The parties first refer a dispute to <roles> for discussion within <period>.
> If unresolved, they may use <agreed mediation process>. This process does not
> prevent urgent relief or restrict rights that cannot lawfully be limited.
> This agreement is governed by the laws of <Australian state or territory>.
> The parties submit to <agreed jurisdiction>, subject to mandatory law.

Complete notices, assignment, variation, order of precedence, severability,
counterparts and authority blocks as appropriate. Execution formalities and forum
fairness require review of the parties, instrument and jurisdiction; no blank
signature block proves authority to execute.
