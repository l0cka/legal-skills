# Map WHS Duties

Lay out every duty holder the facts engage and cite the provision for each.
The map identifies duties; it never concludes that a duty has been breached or
met, or what is reasonably practicable.

Read the [source and control method](../../references/whs-source-and-control-method.md)
first and work from the [WHS framework reference](../../references/whs-framework.md).

## Workflow

1. Fix the arrangement.
   - Record the business or undertaking, its entity type, each workplace and
     the State or Territory where the work is carried out, the workforce
     (employees, contractors, subcontractors, labour hire, volunteers), any
     high-risk work or licensed activity, and the question asked.
   - Load an approved employment profile if one exists and record its version
     and as-at date. Without one, proceed on stated assumptions labelled as
     such; never block the map on a missing profile.
2. Select the governing Act for each workplace.
   - Use the jurisdiction table. Record the claimed basis for any
     Commonwealth Act coverage (Commonwealth entity, public authority or
     Comcare licensed self-insurer). Where work crosses jurisdictions, map
     each one separately.
   - For Victoria, switch to the OHS Act 2004 concepts (employer,
     self-employed person, officer liability). Never map a model WHS Act
     section onto Victoria.
3. Map the duty holders.
   - For each PCBU or employer: its primary duty provision, the workers and
     other persons it is owed to, and any upstream duty (designer,
     manufacturer, importer, supplier, installer) the facts engage.
   - Where more than one person holds the same duty (principal and
     contractor, host and labour hire provider, landlord and tenant), record
     the shared duty and the duty to consult, cooperate and coordinate.
   - For officers: name each role the facts describe as a possible officer
     and the officer provision; record officer status as a question, never a
     conclusion.
   - For workers and other persons: their own duties.
   - Consultation: duties to consult workers, health and safety
     representatives and work groups.
4. Flag engaged regulations and codes.
   - List the regulation parts and approved codes of practice or compliance
     codes the described hazards engage (for example psychosocial hazards,
     working at height, plant, hazardous chemicals), each labelled as
     regulation or code with its version.
   - Record any model-law amendment as model law, not law in force, unless
     the jurisdiction's enactment is verified.
5. Verify.
   - Route every Act, regulation, section and commencement relied on to the
     jurisdiction's checker and record the evidence state. Treat Safe Work
     Australia and regulator pages as guidance with their last-updated dates.
6. Report using the result contract and name the responsible lawyer.

## Result contract

Lead with `READY FOR HUMAN REVIEW`, `READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS`, `NOT READY`
or `OUTSIDE SCOPE`.

```text
Arrangement: <undertaking; entity type; workplaces and jurisdictions; workforce; high-risk work>
Profile: <ID, version, status, as-at date, or "none: assumptions labelled">
Governing Act: <workplace; Act; basis claimed; checker used; evidence state>
Duty map: <duty holder; role; provision; owed to; shared with; source;
  evidence state; checked date>
Officer questions: <role; why it may be an officer; provision; status: question only>
Consultation: <duty; who must be consulted; provision>
Regulations and codes: <part or code; status (regulation, approved code, model law, guidance); version>
Gaps: <unknown facts; unverified propositions; resolver>
Human decision gate: <responsible lawyer; decisions required>
Limitations: <map only; no breach, reasonably practicable or officer-status conclusion>
```

## Fail closed

Return `NOT READY` when neither the undertaking nor the State or Territory
where the work is carried out can be identified or reasonably assumed. Route
workers compensation, criminal defence strategy, coronial matters and the
specialist safety regimes in the method to `OUTSIDE SCOPE` with the
specialist named. Never conclude a breach, never decide what is reasonably
practicable, never conclude that a person is or is not an officer, never apply
one jurisdiction's provision to another, and never present a code of practice
or model-law amendment as the law in force.
