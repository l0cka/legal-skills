# Governance basis

## Authority and currency

This public workflow was prepared for Australian legal assistance services and
reviewed on 14 August 2026. It provides workflow controls, not a statement of a
centre's legal or accreditation compliance.

Use the centre's current approved policies, applicable professional obligations,
funding requirements and authorised sector materials as controlling sources.
Do not reproduce restricted Community Legal Centres Australia materials in a
public profile or plugin.

## Public sources informing the design

- [Community Legal Centres Australia accreditation](https://clcs.org.au/services/accreditation-2/)
  describes the National Accreditation Scheme as a sector quality-assurance
  process covering governance, service delivery, accessibility and client
  feedback. Obtain the detailed standards through authorised centre channels.
- [Community Legal Centres Australia Data Dictionary and Glossary](https://clcs.org.au/services/data/data-dictionary-and-glossary/)
  aligns sector definitions across data guidance, CLASS documentation and the
  Risk Management Guide. Use current authorised CLASS definitions when mapping
  a triage result into a client system.
- [OAIC guidance on commercially available AI products](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/guidance-on-privacy-and-the-use-of-commercially-available-ai-products)
  requires a cautious, purpose-limited approach to personal information,
  transparency, data minimisation, security, accuracy and human oversight. It
  recommends against entering personal or sensitive information into publicly
  available generative-AI tools.
- [National AI Centre Guidance for AI Adoption](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/guidance-for-ai-adoption)
  provides current Australian responsible-AI governance practices. Apply them
  proportionately and assign an accountable owner for the deployed workflow.
- [Justice Connect's intake tool](https://justiceconnect.org.au/innovation/legal-help-experience/intake-tool/)
  shows the value of multiple access channels, co-design, tailored pathways and
  evaluation. Its [AI project](https://justiceconnect.org.au/innovation/legal-help-experience/ai-project/)
  also identifies bias risks in legal-problem classification and distinguishes
  classification from decision-making.

Recheck these sources before each plugin release and before using them for a
centre's approval decision.

## Required centre review

Obtain review from people accountable for the legal practice, intake operations,
privacy or information governance, accessibility and cultural safety. Include
frontline staff and people with lived experience in testing where the centre can
do so safely and ethically.

Test with synthetic cases before any client information is used. Measure missed
urgent matters, erroneous scope exclusions, inconsistent outcomes, inaccessible
questions, referral failures and differences affecting priority communities.
